Route the crabs.
Beat the tide.

A fast, kid-friendly crab-routing game for 1 to 6 players. The tide is out: place arrows in the sand to steer streams of crabs into your castle before the sea, and the gulls, take everything back.

Download Source on GitHub

Free and open source, Apache-2.0 · Linux, macOS, Windows · Built in Rust with Bevy

One verb, and a beach that fights back

Built on the mechanical skeleton of ChuChu Rocket! (1999): creatures walk forward, turn at walls by a fixed rule, and arrows are the only thing you get to place. Everything below is what this one does differently.

Six players, not four

Four corner castles plus two mid-edge on the wider arenas, with team play that grows with the table: 2v2 at four, 2v2v2 or 3v3 at six.

Every crab has a handedness

Left-clawed crabs try left first, right-clawed right, with the oversized claw as the tell. One bit of state that turns herding puzzles into sorting ones: the same corridor routes two crabs to two destinations.

The score is on the board

Castles grow through four tiers as they bank. The HUD is the redundancy, not the readout.

A raid costs half, and hands some back

A gull carries off half a castle's bank and spills live crabs onto the sand for everyone to scramble over. A raid drains the flock instead of compounding it.

The tide is the clock

The sea closing in around the board is the timer. The last 30 seconds double the gull spawn rate; the wave stops everything where it stands.

Deterministic to the bit

The simulation is engine-free and integer-only, which is what lets lockstep netcode, replays, a serverless daily challenge and the level solver all share one implementation.

Eight ways to play

Tide Pool

A 100-level solo puzzle campaign. The test suite proves every level solvable with the arrows it grants, and unsolvable without them.

Turf War

Local versus for 2 to 6 on one keyboard plus gamepads. Six maps, team play, a best-of-3 or 5 series, and AI at three levels that walks a cursor to the tile the way you do.

Beach Lobby

Up to six players over LAN on deterministic UDP lockstep, with host-relay star and state-hash desync detection. Hosts announce themselves; the lobby lists every beach it can hear.

Driftwood

A level editor with a built-in solver that proves a level beatable before it ships. Saved levels join the campaign and can be played as versus beaches.

Beach Day

Eight score-attack challenge stages: timed goals under versus rules.

Replay

Every finished round is kept and watchable at 1x, 2x or 4x. Rounds copy out as checksummed share codes, including one still in progress.

Daily Challenge

One date-seeded arena per day, identical worldwide, with a local best. No server: the simulation is the same everywhere.

Achievements

Fifty trophies and lifetime stats, on a shelf that scrolls.

Prebuilt binary

Linux, macOS and Windows, on x86_64 and arm64. Unpack and run; there is no installer and no launcher.

Latest release

From crates.io

With a Rust toolchain installed:

cargo install --locked pinch-points

From source

git clone https://github.com/agourlay/pinch-points
cd pinch-points
cargo build --release

The last two need Bevy's Linux build dependencies: libudev-dev, libasound2-dev and libwayland-dev or the X11 equivalents.

The full guide has the rules, every mode in depth, all the controls and settings, and the development hooks. The design document explains why the game is shaped the way it is.
